# Edge Geometry — The Thesis

The founding observation is a convergence: systems built independently,
for different consumers, keep arriving at the same boundary from different
sides.

## Four Sides of One Edge

1. **Schubert — dynamics.** A wall is the parameter value at which a
   capability crosses from stable to unstable: from something to nothing.
   The wall-crossing engine computes phase diagrams; the wall is the
   existence boundary. Schubert's probe S4 measured the trust×time
   product of walls and found the current engine *additive* — per-wall,
   non-interacting.

2. **Karpal — kinds.** The Ω lattice types emptiness: `StructuralZero`
   (overdrawn budget), `GeometricZero` (dimensioned but empty),
   `Positive` (exists, with multiplicity), `Underdetermined` (epistemic).
   The provenance of a zero is load-bearing — a denial for lack of budget
   is a different fact than a denial despite budget.

3. **The topos — classification.** Ω is being built into a subobject
   classifier: categorically, *the* interface between a subobject and its
   complement. The general object already exists in this frame; the
   question is whether Thatch's geometry is a sibling or a stratified,
   ε-thickened instance of it.

4. **Quantizon — descent.** The gluing-failure table asks whether local
   boundary data composes to global data: descent for the edge.

## The Thesis Statement

**The infinitesimal boundary between something and emptiness (or a
singularity) is itself a geometric object.** It has strata, it carries
structure, and it composes when two somethings share an edge.

"Composes" is the operative word, and the reason Thatch lives beside
operads and wall-crossing rather than inside point-set topology. When two
grants share a capability, when two queries share a boundary condition,
when two somethings abut — the interface they share is a *third* object,
and its geometry is not determined by either side alone.

## The Established Fields

The founding document surveys six bodies of mathematics that already
study pieces of this picture — evidence the question is real:

1. **Logarithmic geometry** — the deformation-to-the-normal-cone
   tradition: log structures record the boundary in the geometry itself.
2. **Formal neighborhoods and normal cones** — the infinitesimal
   thickening of a subvariety.
3. **KS wall-crossing** — the dynamical side: how invariants transform
   across walls of marginal stability.
4. **Schubert singular strata and resolution** — Bott–Samelson
   resolutions: the stratified structure of the boundary.
5. **Skeletons and shadows** — the combinatorial cores that retain the
   intersection theory.
6. **Surreal infinitesimals** — in-stack, concrete: numbers smaller than
   every positive real, available as thickening parameters.

Thatch does not import these; it stays combinatorial and decidable. The
fields name the neighborhoods where the geometry would live if it grows
beyond the poset.

## What This Is Not

Thatch is not a general-purpose geometry library, not a theorem prover,
and not (yet) a formalization of any of the six fields. It is a small,
verified toolbox for the *combinatorial* geometry of the edge — strata,
posets, and the emptiness lattice — with falsifiable probes marking the
path from here to the deeper theory. See
[the probe program](../design/probes.md).
